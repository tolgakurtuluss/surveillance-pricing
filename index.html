What this demonstrates

Traditional airline revenue management primarily prices the seat: it reacts to booking curves, remaining inventory and time-to-departure. This simulator illustrates a different mechanism — pricing the person by allowing shopper-level signals to alter the number shown before seat selection.

The attribution view makes the mechanism explicit: some signals are modeled as willingness-to-pay uplifts, while others act as conversion incentives. The case comparison then shows how six passenger profiles could land on different numbers for the same underlying inventory state.

Educational simulation only. The weights are illustrative and are not taken from any airline's actual pricing engine. The model is intended to support discussion of surveillance pricing, personalization, transparency and the boundary between conventional RM and shopper-level pricing.
